# Volume 2 Part C: The Driver

The Australian Freight Playbook · Volume 2 · November 2026 edition · figures correct as at Nov 20, 2026

## Before You Start

This part is for the person behind the wheel. If you own one truck and drive it yourself, it is also your first compliance manual, because in a small business the driver, the scheduler and the boss are often the same person.

The law treats you as more than the person holding the steering wheel. You answer for your own fitness, your hours, your load, your vehicle and the records that prove it. The good news is that the rules follow a pattern, and once you know the pattern you can work anywhere in Australia.

**How to read it**

- Six states and territories run the Heavy Vehicle National Law (HVNL): NSW, VIC, QLD, SA, TAS and the ACT. A big set of changes started on **1 August 2026**.
- WA and the NT run their own laws. Where they differ, you will see a separate line for them.
- Every figure here was correct when this edition was checked. Laws, fines and rates change, so check the source listed before you rely on a number in court or in a contract.
- Lines marked **Check before print** are still being confirmed against the official source.

This part explains the law in plain words. It is not legal advice and it is not accredited training.

## Chapter 1: Your Licence

Your licence class sets the biggest vehicle you may drive, and every step up needs time on the class below. The classes are national, so a licence issued in one state is good in all of them.

| Class | What it lets you drive | What you need first (NSW) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C | Cars, utes and vans up to 4.5 t GVM | Full car licence |
| LR, Light Rigid | Rigid trucks over 4.5 t up to 8 t GVM; buses over 12 seats up to 8 t. Trailer up to 9 t GVM | C held 1 year |
| MR, Medium Rigid | Rigid trucks over 8 t GVM with 2 axles. Trailer up to 9 t GVM | C held 1 year |
| HR, Heavy Rigid | Rigid trucks over 8 t GVM with 3 or more axles. Trailer up to 9 t GVM | C held 2 years |
| HC, Heavy Combination | Semi trailers, and rigids towing a trailer over 9 t GVM | Unrestricted MR or HR held 1 year |
| MC, Multi Combination | B doubles, road trains, low loader with dolly | Unrestricted HR or HC held 1 year, plus a medical |

Source: [NSW Government](https://www.nsw.gov.au/driving-boating-and-transport/driver-and-rider-licences/heavy-vehicle-licences/getting-a-heavy-vehicle-licence). The classes are the same everywhere, but each state adds its own conditions:

| State or territory | What is different |
| --- | --- |
| VIC | All heavy vehicle testing is done by DTP accredited training providers. A formal training course is mandatory for HC and MC |
| QLD | Licence tenure counts if held within the last 5 years. TMR tests LR to HC; MC is only through an approved RTO, with at least 16 hours of training and employer sign off |
| SA | HR and HC from age 19, MC from 20. HC and MC only through competency based training. The MC Licence Program lets an employer sponsor an HR or HC driver to MC with 50 to 60 hours of supervised driving |
| WA | HC and MC need a car licence held 3 years as well as 1 year on the class below. HR to MC licences cannot be provisional. RTO assessments are recorded on video and GPS |
| TAS | HR and HC from age 20, MC from 21 with an MR68 medical. A driver with a car licence held 2 years can go straight to an HC learner licence through a course |
| ACT | A commercial driver health assessment (CDHA) is needed for MR and above. MC needs a mandatory course |
| NT | Practical tests are in a vehicle loaded to at least 75%. HC and MC need an approved training course, and the MC test runs on a road train route |

The state rows come from the November 2026 research dossier and are **Check before print** against each licensing agency.

**Getting the upgrade in NSW.** You pass an eyesight test and the Heavy Vehicle Knowledge Test, then either a driving test or the Heavy Vehicle Competency Based Assessment (HVCBA) with a registered training organisation. MC can only be done through the HVCBA, and an HR holder must pass an HC assessment before MC training starts.

**What is coming.** Transport ministers approved changes to the national driver competency framework. Austroads is phasing them in over several years, with proposed minimum training of 20.5 hours for LR and MR up to 25.5 hours for MC, including set behind the wheel time ([Austroads](https://austroads.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0034/616966/NHVDCF_Factsheet1_Competencies_and_Training.pdf)). Those hours may change after trials.

**What puts your licence at risk**

- Demerit points: speeding and phone offences cost the same points in a truck as in a car, and your living depends on that licence.
- Any alcohol over your state's heavy vehicle limit (Chapter 2).
- Drug driving: every state runs roadside drug tests.
- Serious fatigue breaches, which can lead to court and a licence sanction.

**Tip for operators:** check every driver's licence class and expiry before their first shift, and again every year. Driving outside your class is the driver's offence, but letting it happen is yours.

## Chapter 2: Am I Fit to Drive Today?

From 1 August 2026, the HVNL says you must not drive a heavy vehicle over 4.5 t if you are unfit to drive it safely, for any reason. That covers fatigue, illness, injury, drugs, alcohol and mental fitness ([Big Rigs](https://bigrigs.com.au/2026/06/30/what-to-know-about-the-amended-hvnl/)). Before this change the duty only covered fatigue.

The same reform tells everyone who schedules, loads or pays you that they must not cause or encourage you to drive while unfit. If you say "I'm not right to drive today", the law is on your side.

**The three question check before every shift**

1. Did I get enough sleep, and do I feel alert?
2. Am I sick, injured, or on medication that warns against driving or operating machinery?
3. Have I had any alcohol or drugs in the last day, or is anything on my mind that would take my attention off the road?

If any answer worries you, tell your scheduler before you start, not halfway through the run.

Answer the three questions honestly. One doubtful answer is enough to stop and speak up; the law puts the duty on you and on anyone who pressures you.

### Alcohol Limits for Heavy Vehicle Drivers

| State or territory | Limit | Applies to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NSW | Under 0.02 | Over 13.9 t GVM or GCM ([NSW Gov](https://www.nsw.gov.au/driving-boating-and-transport/roads-safety-and-rules/safe-driving/alcohol-and-drugs)) |
| VIC | Zero | Over 4.5 t GVM, since 1 Apr 2021 ([Transport VIC](https://transport.vic.gov.au/road-and-active-transport/business-and-industry/heavy-vehicles/heavy-vehicles-road-safety/zero-bac-requirement-for-all-heavy-vehicle-drivers)) |
| QLD | Zero | Any truck over 4.5 t GVM, articulated vehicles, placard DG loads ([QLD Gov](https://www.qld.gov.au/transport/safety/road-safety/drink-driving/blood-alcohol)) |
| SA | Zero | Over 15 t GVM ([Austroads](https://austroads.gov.au/publications/assessing-fitness-to-drive/ap-g56/drivers-legal-bac-limits)) |
| TAS | Zero | Goods vehicles over 4.5 t GVM ([Tas legislation](https://legislation.tas.gov.au/view/pdf/authorised/2022-03-01%202022-03-10/act-1970-077)) |
| ACT | Zero | Over 15 t GVM or GCM ([Austroads](https://austroads.gov.au/publications/assessing-fitness-to-drive/ap-g56/drivers-legal-bac-limits)) |
| WA | Zero | Over 22.5 t GCM ([WA Road Safety Commission](https://www.wa.gov.au/organisation/road-safety-commission/drink-and-drug-driving)) |
| NT | Zero | Over 15 t GVM ([Austroads](https://austroads.gov.au/publications/assessing-fitness-to-drive/ap-g56/drivers-legal-bac-limits)) |

**The simple rule:** in any heavy vehicle, in any state, drink nothing. A light truck may sit under the state's zero threshold, but the new unfit to drive duty still applies if alcohol affects you, and most employers run their own zero policy anyway.

**Drugs.** Police in every state and territory run roadside drug tests, and there is no safe level for illegal drugs. Prescription and over the counter medicines count too: if the label warns about drowsiness or operating machinery, ask your doctor or pharmacist before you drive.

**Medicals.** Heavy vehicle drivers are measured against Austroads' Assessing Fitness to Drive. Stricter commercial standards apply to MR, HR, HC and MC drivers, dangerous goods drivers and drivers under fatigue accreditation; LR drivers usually fall under the private standard. Sleep apnoea, diabetes, heart conditions, hearing and some eye conditions must be declared.

Routine medicals by state for heavy vehicle licence holders ([Austroads](https://austroads.gov.au/publications/assessing-fitness-to-drive/ap-g56/regulatory-requirements-for-driver-testing)):

| State or territory | Periodic medical | Extra for MC |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NSW | Every year from age 75; road test every year from 80 | Medical at first application, at 21, every 10 years to 40, every 5 years to 60, every 2 years to 70, then yearly. Road test yearly from 70 |
| VIC | None by age; only when a condition is declared or reported | None listed |
| QLD | From 75, a medical certificate valid for a maximum of 1 year, carried while driving | None listed |
| SA | Every year from 70 | South of Port Augusta: every 3 years to age 49, then yearly |
| TAS | None by age | Medical at first application |
| ACT | Commercial medical from 75, then yearly. The research dossier reports newer Access Canberra rules requiring CDHAs every 5 years to 70 for MR and above: **Check before print** | None listed |
| WA | Yearly from 80, road test from 85 | None listed. But every commercial driver needs a current medical certificate under WA fatigue rules ([WorkSafe WA](https://www.worksafe.wa.gov.au/publications/fact-sheet-driving-commercial-vehicles)) |
| NT | Only when a condition is reported | None listed |

Operators with fatigue accreditation (ACH, or legacy BFM or AFM) must also have their drivers medically assessed against the commercial standard, reported as every 3 years to age 49 and yearly from 50. **Check before print.**

## Chapter 3: Work and Rest Hours

If you drive a fatigue regulated heavy vehicle in an HVNL state, the law caps your work and sets minimum rest in rolling windows of time. A fatigue regulated heavy vehicle is a truck or combination over 12 t GVM or GCM, a bus over 4.5 t built for more than 12 adults, or a truck over 12 t with a machine attached ([NHVR](https://wws.nhvr.gov.au/safety-accreditation-compliance/fatigue-management/record-keeping-requirements)).

"Work" means more than driving. Loading, unloading, paperwork, refuelling, cleaning and inspecting the truck all count as work time.

### Standard Hours for Solo Drivers

| In any period of | Maximum work | Minimum rest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5.5 hours | 5.25 hours | 15 continuous minutes |
| 8 hours | 7.5 hours | 30 minutes, in blocks of 15 |
| 11 hours | 10 hours | 60 minutes, in blocks of 15 |
| 24 hours | 12 hours | 7 continuous hours stationary rest |
| 7 days | 72 hours | 24 continuous hours stationary rest |
| 14 days | 144 hours | 2 night rest breaks, plus 2 night rest breaks on consecutive days |

&#91;embedded content: Solo standard hours · NHVR · unchanged on 1 August 2026\]

Notice the 24 hour bar: half of every day must be time off work, and 7 hours of it in one unbroken stretch.

Source: [NHVR](https://www.nhvr.gov.au/work-and-rest). These numbers did not change on 1 August 2026. A night rest break is 7 continuous hours of stationary rest taken between 10 pm and 8 am. Stationary rest means out of the truck, or in an approved sleeper berth while the truck is parked.

### Standard Hours for Two Up Drivers

Two drivers sharing a truck with an approved sleeper berth can rest while the truck moves, but their weekly limits are lower ([NHVR](https://www.nhvr.gov.au/work-and-rest)):

| In any period of | Maximum work | Minimum rest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5.5 hours | 5.25 hours | 15 continuous minutes |
| 8 hours | 7.5 hours | 30 minutes, in blocks of 15 |
| 11 hours | 10 hours | 60 minutes, in blocks of 15 |
| 24 hours | 12 hours | 5 continuous hours stationary rest, or 5 continuous hours in an approved sleeper berth while moving |
| 52 hours | No set limit | 10 continuous hours stationary rest |
| 7 days | 60 hours | 24 continuous hours stationary rest, plus 24 hours in blocks of at least 7 continuous hours |
| 14 days | 120 hours | 2 night rest breaks, plus 2 night rest breaks on consecutive days |

Rest in a moving sleeper berth only counts if it comes before the driver's major rest break.

**Reading the table the way an inspector does.** The windows roll. Any 5.5 hours you pick, from any starting minute, must contain no more than 5.25 hours of work. Most breaches happen when drivers count from the start of the shift instead of checking every window.

### Alternative Compliance Hours (ACH)

From 1 August 2026, the old Basic Fatigue Management (BFM) and Advanced Fatigue Management (AFM) options are replaced by Alternative Compliance Hours, run under the new Heavy Vehicle Accreditation scheme. ACH lets an accredited operator use longer or more flexible hours, including NHVR template tables based on the old BFM schedules. Existing BFM and AFM accreditations stay valid until they expire. If you drive for an accredited operator, ask which hours you are working under, and carry proof.

### WA and the NT

- **WA:** fatigue sits under the Work Health and Safety (General) Regulations 2022, Part 4.10, plus a code of practice. It catches more vehicles than the HVNL: any goods vehicle over **4.5 t GVM** carrying for hire or reward, when the driver works more than 60 hours a week, more than 10 hours in 24 on more than one day a week, or between midnight and 5 am more than once a week ([WorkSafe WA](https://www.worksafe.wa.gov.au/publications/fact-sheet-driving-commercial-vehicles)).
- **NT:** there are no set hours. Fatigue is managed as a general work health and safety duty. Once you cross into an HVNL state, the HVNL table applies; if any part of the trip is in WA, the WA rules apply for the whole trip ([NT Gov](https://nt.gov.au/driving/industry/fatigue-management/operating-across-borders)).

WA limits for a solo driver ([WorkSafe WA](https://www.worksafe.wa.gov.au/publications/fact-sheet-driving-commercial-vehicles)):

| Rule | Limit |
| --- | --- |
| Breaks from driving | 20 minutes off driving for every 5 hours of work, including at least 10 continuous minutes |
| Daily | No more than 17 hours between 7 hour non work periods |
| Any 72 hours | At least 27 hours non work, including three blocks of 7 continuous hours |
| Any 14 days | No more than 168 hours of work |
| Weekly rest | Two 24 hour non work periods in any 14 days, or four in any 28 days with no more than 144 hours of work in any 14 of them |
| Records | Work, breaks and non work time, kept for 3 years |

In WA a break of less than 30 minutes still counts as work time. The 72 hour rule means three 17 hour days in a row are never legal.

**Tool:** T14 Trip and hours planner checks a planned run against these windows before you leave. It plans; it never replaces your diary.

## Chapter 4: Work Diaries and Records

Your records are your proof. If an inspector cannot see that you rested, the law assumes you did not.

### When You Need a Work Diary (HVNL States)

You must carry a work diary when driving a fatigue regulated heavy vehicle if ([NHVR](https://wws.nhvr.gov.au/safety-accreditation-compliance/fatigue-management/record-keeping-requirements)):

- you drive 100 km or more from your base on standard hours, or did so in the last 28 days, or
- you work under accredited or exemption hours (ACH, or a transitional BFM or AFM accreditation), or did so in the last 28 days.

### Local Work: Within 100 km of Base

No diary is needed, but a local area record is. It shows the dates you drove, each vehicle's rego, and your daily and weekly totals of work and rest. Fill it in within 24 hours of finishing work for the day and hand it to your record keeper within 21 days. The record keeper keeps it for three years. If you are self employed, you are your own record keeper, so you also keep your rosters, trip schedules and payment records.

### Written or Electronic

- **Written work diary:** bought from your state licensing agency (in NSW, from Service NSW, in person with your licence) ([Service NSW](https://www.service.nsw.gov.au/transaction/request-a-national-driver-work-diary-log-book)). From 1 August 2026 the daily sheets are simpler, and old stock can be used until it runs out ([Big Rigs](https://bigrigs.com.au/2026/06/30/what-to-know-about-the-amended-hvnl/)). Written diaries count in 15 minute blocks.
- **Electronic work diary (EWD):** an approved app or device, usually a paid subscription through your operator. EWDs count to the minute. The amended rules give EWD users a small tolerance on maximum work time (reported as 8 minutes). **Check before print.**

### Five Habits That Keep You Out of Trouble

1. Fill in each line as it happens, not at the end of the day.
2. Record the location where every break starts and ends.
3. Write the odometer reading at the start and end of each shift.
4. Keep the duplicate pages; never tear out a page.
5. When you cross a border, start or keep the diary as the matrix in Chapter 11 says.

WA keeps its own record system for commercial drivers, and the NT does not require a national diary inside the NT. Both are covered in Chapter 11.

## Chapter 5: Your Load Is Your Problem Too

The consignor, the loader and the packer can all be fined for a bad load, but so can you. Once you drive away, an overloaded axle or a loose pallet is a driver offence as well.

### Three Things You Answer For

1. **Mass.** The vehicle, each axle group and the combination must be within their limits, and never above the manufacturer's ratings. From 1 August 2026, General Mass Limits absorbed the old Concessional Mass Limits: up to 1 t more for combinations of 55 t or less and 2 t more above 55 t, with no permit or accreditation needed ([Big Rigs](https://bigrigs.com.au/2026/06/30/what-to-know-about-the-amended-hvnl/)). Higher Mass Limits still need accreditation and certified road friendly suspension.
2. **Dimension.** Height, width, length and overhang. General access length went from 19 m to 20 m on 1 August 2026, if wheelbase and equipment rules are met.
3. **Restraint.** The load must not move, spill or fall. The law sets how much force your restraint must hold ([NHVR](https://www.nhvr.gov.au/road-access/loading/loading-performance-standards)):

| Direction | Must hold | When it bites |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Forward | 0.8 g, or 80% of the load's weight | Hard braking |
| Rearward | 0.5 g, or 50% | Accelerating, hills |
| Sideways | 0.5 g, or 50%, each side | Cornering, swerving |
| Upward | 0.2 g, or 20% | Bumps; applies when your restraint relies on friction |

Weight alone never holds a load. A tall item like a fridge or a safe can tip forward under hard braking even when it is heavy, so block it against the headboard or lash it directly.

Common axle group limits under the new General Mass Limits, as reported in the research dossier: steer 6.0 t (6.5 t for complying vehicles), single axle dual tyres 9.0 t, tandem dual tyres 17.0 t, tri axle dual tyres 21.0 t. **Check before print** against the NHVR General Mass Limits page.

### Before You Sign for a Load

- Compare the paperwork weight with what is on the deck. If a pallet looks heavier than declared, ask.
- Look at how the load is placed. Heavy items low and over the axles; nothing leaning on the gates.
- Count and check your straps, chains, edge protectors and anti slip mats. Frayed straps fail.
- For containers, check the Container Weight Declaration matches the container number.
- Recheck restraint after the first few kilometres and at every stop. Loads settle.

### When the Load Is Wrong

Say so before you leave, and write it down: who you told, the time, and what they said. You can refuse to drive an unsafe or overloaded vehicle. Chapter 7 explains why you are protected when you do.

&#91;embedded content: Wrong load · 2 decisions\]

Speak up at the dock, not at the roadside. A note of who you told and when is your record if anything goes wrong later.

**Tools:** T11 Axle load and pallet placement and T13 Load restraint calculator (Part D) help you plan. They do not replace a weighbridge or the Load Restraint Guide.

## Chapter 6: Vehicle Condition and Speed

A defect you knew about, or should have found on your pre start, is your problem as well as the owner's.

### The Daily Pre Start

Walk around the truck before every shift and record what you checked. A good pre start covers:

- Tyres (pressure, damage, tread) and wheel nuts
- Lights, indicators, reflectors and number plates
- Brakes, air system leaks and the park brake
- Couplings, turntable, kingpin, safety chains and dollies
- Mirrors, windscreen, wipers and horn
- Fluid leaks under the truck
- Restraint gear, gates and curtains
- Fire extinguisher, first aid kit and any DG equipment

If something fails, report it in writing and do not drive until it is fixed or cleared. The Part D forms pack has a pre start and defect report.

### Defect Notices

In the HVNL states, a fault found at the roadside leads to one of these, from lightest to heaviest:

| Notice | What it means | What you do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Formal warning | Low risk, first time | Fix it; no inspection |
| Self clearing defect notice | Not a safety risk (a dirty plate, a minor fault) | Fix it within the time given; it clears itself. Not used in WA or the NT |
| Minor defect notice | A safety risk, but not immediate | Keep driving under the conditions on the notice, then have it inspected and cleared by the deadline |
| Major defect notice | Immediate and serious risk; a label goes on the truck | Drive only where the notice allows, such as to a repairer |
| Major defect, grounded | Not to be driven at all | Repair on the spot or tow it |

If you are not the owner, give the notice to the owner or operator as soon as you can, and within 14 days. Clearing a notice is run by each state: NSW uses authorised inspection stations, VIC uses licensed heavy vehicle testers, QLD uses TMR sites or approved stations, SA and the ACT use government stations only. WA uses its own "yellow sticker" defect notice, and the NT clears notices only at government inspection facilities. These details come from the research dossier: **Check before print**, including any fees.

### Speed

- **NSW:** any vehicle over 4.5 t GVM is limited to 100 km/h, even on a 110 km/h road ([NSW Gov](https://www.nsw.gov.au/driving-boating-and-transport/roads-safety-and-rules/heavy-vehicles/speed-limits)).
- **VIC:** the government says heavy vehicles may not go faster than 100 km/h anywhere in Victoria ([Transport VIC](https://transport.vic.gov.au/road-and-active-transport/road-rules-and-safety/truck-and-heavy-vehicle-road-rules)). The research dossier reports that the road rule itself sets the cap at over 12 t GVM. **Check before print.**
- **ACT, TAS and WA:** vehicles over 12 t GVM are capped at 100 km/h ([NHVR](https://www.nhvr.gov.au/road-access/mass-dimension-and-loading/classes-of-heavy-vehicles/class-2/road-train-speed-limits)).
- **QLD, SA and NT:** the 100 km/h cap applies to vehicles over 12 t GVM (and buses over 5 t), per the research dossier. A 4.5 t to 12 t truck may legally do the posted limit, up to 130 km/h on some NT highways. **Check before print.**
- **Speed limiters:** trucks over 15 t GVM, and trucks over 12 t GVM built from 1 January 1991, must be limited to 100 km/h ([NSW Gov](https://www.nsw.gov.au/driving-boating-and-transport/roads-safety-and-rules/heavy-vehicles/speed-limits)). Tampering with a limiter is a serious offence for the driver and the operator.
- **Road trains:** lower limits apply in some states, for example 90 km/h in NSW under the Class 2 notice and 90 km/h in QLD for everything except B triples.

**Best practice for any fleet:** set a company rule of 100 km/h for every truck over 4.5 t, in every state. It keeps you legal in NSW the moment you cross the border, and a loaded 8 t truck at 110 km/h stops and corners worse than the speed sign assumes.

The limit that applies is always the lowest of the road rules, your notice or permit, the road conditions and the signs.

## Chapter 7: Chain of Responsibility from the Cab

Chain of Responsibility (CoR) means the people behind the truck share the blame when their decisions push a driver into breaking the law. It exists to stop the driver being the only one who pays.

### Who Is in the Chain

Under the HVNL, the chain includes the employer, prime contractor, operator, scheduler, consignor, consignee, loading manager, loader, unloader and packer. Each must do all that is reasonably practicable to make sure their part of the transport task is safe. Company directors and managers carry their own duty to make sure the business complies.

The driver is not one of these parties, because the driver already carries direct duties: hours, fitness, load, vehicle and speed. CoR is what protects you from pressure that comes from above.

### What Others Cannot Ask of You

It is an offence for anyone to ask, direct or contract you in a way they know, or ought to know, would make you:

- speed,
- drive while tired or unfit,
- break your work and rest hours, or
- drive an overloaded, oversize or badly restrained vehicle.

The 1 August 2026 reform widened this rule to cover any heavy vehicle over 4.5 t and roughly doubled the penalty. For 1 August 2026 to 30 June 2027 the maximum is **$28,230** for an individual, court only, with no on the spot fine ([NHVR penalty schedule](https://www.nhvr.gov.au/document/910)). A company can face up to five times that. Schedulers, dispatchers and clients who set impossible delivery windows are exactly who this is aimed at.

### Pressure Looks Like This

- A delivery window you cannot make without speeding or skipping a break.
- "Just this once, don't write it down."
- Pay that only works if you run over hours.
- Being told to wait unpaid at a dock, then make up the time on the road.
- A load you are told to take even though it is clearly over.

### What to Do

1. Say no calmly, and give the reason: "I can't make that time legally."
2. Write it down: who asked, when, what was said.
3. Keep texts and emails.
4. Report it to the NHVR's **Heavy Vehicle Confidential Reporting Line: 1800 931 785**, Monday to Friday, 7 am to 4.30 pm AEST ([NHVR](https://www.nhvr.gov.au/hvcrl)). You can stay anonymous. For anything dangerous happening right now, such as a crash or a load falling off, call 000.

Saying no and writing it down are worth doing every time, even if you decide not to report. Your note is what turns "he said, she said" into evidence.

A client who sets impossible times is breaking the law, not you for refusing them.

## Chapter 8: Roadside Intercepts and Penalties

A roadside check goes quickly when your paperwork is in order and your answers are short and true.

### Who Can Pull You Over

| Where | Who checks heavy vehicles |
| --- | --- |
| NSW, VIC, QLD, SA, TAS, ACT | NHVR Safety and Compliance Officers and state or territory police |
| WA | Main Roads WA heavy vehicle inspectors and WA Police; WorkSafe WA for fatigue |
| NT | Motor Vehicle Registry officers and NT Police |

### What They Can Ask For

- Your licence
- Your work diary or local area record
- Any permit, notice or accreditation you are working under (carry a copy)
- Load and consignment paperwork, including a Container Weight Declaration
- A look over the vehicle, and a weigh on a scale or weighbridge
- A breath test and a drug test

### At the Stop

1. Pull over where it is safe, engine off if asked.
2. Hand over what is asked for. Keep your documents together in one folder so you are not searching.
3. Answer questions about facts. You can say you want advice before answering anything about fault.
4. Read anything before you sign it, and ask for a copy.
5. Note the officer's name or number, the time and the place.

### How Penalties Work

Most HVNL breaches are graded by risk: minor, substantial, severe or critical. The bigger the breach, the bigger the fine, and the more likely it goes to court instead of an on the spot infringement. Breaking hours by a few minutes is minor; by more than an hour or so it can become severe or critical.

Penalties are indexed every 1 July. For 1 August 2026 to 30 June 2027 ([NHVR penalty schedule](https://www.nhvr.gov.au/document/910)):

| Offence | Maximum (court) | On the spot fine | Demerit points |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Standard hours breach, minor risk | $4,230 | $423 | 0 |
| Standard hours breach, substantial risk | $8,480 | $848 | 0 |
| Standard hours breach, severe risk | $14,120 | $1,412 | 3 |
| Standard hours breach, critical risk | $21,170 | Court only | 4 |
| Driving while fatigued or unfit (s 228) | $28,230 | Court only | 3 |
| Asking or contracting a driver to break the law (s 26E) | $28,230 | Court only | None |

An on the spot fine is 10% of the maximum, and only offences with a maximum of $14,120 or less get one. Anything bigger goes to court.

If you receive an infringement you think is wrong, you can ask for a review or elect to go to court. Get advice first: in a heavy vehicle, a conviction can follow you into every job application.

## Chapter 9: Pay and Contracts

How you are paid depends first on one question: are you an employee or a contractor? The answer sets which laws protect you.

### Employee Drivers

Two federal awards cover most truck drivers:

- **Road Transport and Distribution Award 2020**: local and general freight work.
- **Road Transport (Long Distance Operations) Award 2020**: long distance runs, including pay by the kilometre or the trip.

They set minimum rates by grade, overtime, allowances and breaks. Rates change every 1 July after the Fair Work Commission's annual wage review, so check the Fair Work Ombudsman's pay guides for current figures. An enterprise agreement may replace the award, but cannot leave you worse off overall.

### Federal Rules for Every State

The Fair Work Act now lets the Fair Work Commission make orders for road transport contractors and contract chains. The first one matters for fuel: the **Road Transport Contractual Chain Order for fuel cost recovery** restarts on **23 November 2026**. Hirers must review your rates at least monthly to cover fuel, or pay an agreed fuel levy, until diesel stays under $2 a litre for four weeks in a row ([King and Wood Mallesons](https://www.mallesons.com/au/en/insights/latest-thinking/the-fair-work-commissions-fuel-cost-recovery-order-is-back.html)). There is no general minimum rates order for owner drivers yet.

### Before You Sign Any Contract, Check

1. The rate, and whether it covers fuel, tolls, waiting time and empty running.
2. Payment terms: how many days after invoice.
3. Notice to end the contract, on both sides.
4. Who pays for damage, fines and insurance excesses.
5. Whether you are free to work for others.

If a business treats you like an employee (sets your hours, supplies the truck, bans other work) but calls you a contractor, get advice. That can be sham contracting.

## Chapter 10: Health on the Road

The truck gets a service schedule; the driver usually does not. Long hours sitting, broken sleep, servo food and time away from home wear people down, and the unfit to drive duty now makes your health a legal matter as well as a personal one.

### Sleep

- Sleep at the same times where you can. Your body clock does not care what the roster says.
- Snoring, gasping at night or nodding off during the day can mean sleep apnoea. It is common in drivers and treatable, and an untreated case can cost you your licence.
- A short nap at a safe stop beats pushing through. Caffeine only buys a little time.

### Food, Water and Movement

- Carry water and drink through the day, more in the north and the inland heat.
- Pack food where you can, so the only choice is not the servo bain marie.
- At every break, walk around the truck and stretch. It doubles as your load check.

### Heat, Cold and Remote Country

- In outback heat, carry extra water for a breakdown, not just for drinking on the move.
- Tell someone your route and expected arrival when you run remote. Mobile coverage drops out on long stretches.
- Know where the next fuel, water and rest area is before you lose signal.

### Your Head

Long stretches alone, money stress and time away from family add up. Talk to someone before it builds:

- **Lifeline:** 13 11 14, any time.
- **Healthy Heads in Trucks and Sheds:** the industry's own mental health foundation, with resources built for drivers.
- Your GP, and any employee assistance program your employer offers.

If you are not right to drive, saying so is the professional call, and the law backs you.

## Chapter 11: Crossing Borders

The rules that bind you are the rules of the place your wheels are on, and some of them reach back over the border with you.

| Crossing | What changes | What to carry |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Between HVNL states (NSW, VIC, QLD, SA, TAS, ACT) | Same national fatigue law. Alcohol limits, speed rules and road train access still differ by state (matrix) | Work diary if you are 100 km or more from base; any notice or permit for each state |
| NT into an HVNL state | The HVNL applies from the border | A national work diary filled in from at least your last major rest break before you crossed. If you were in the NT for less than seven days, keep the diary for the whole period ([NT Gov](https://nt.gov.au/driving/industry/fatigue-management/operating-across-borders)) |
| Any trip touching WA | WA's own fatigue system applies for the whole trip, not just the WA part | A WA compliant work record for the entire trip, plus your medical certificate ([NT Gov](https://nt.gov.au/driving/industry/fatigue-management/operating-across-borders), [WorkSafe WA](https://www.worksafe.wa.gov.au/fatigue-management-commercial-vehicle-drivers)) |
| Into WA as a business operator | Main Roads WA accreditation (WAHVA) if your task is heavy, oversize or under permit, for interstate operators too ([Main Roads WA](https://mainroads.wa.gov.au/heavy-vehicles/accreditation-auditing)) | Accreditation details and any RAV permit or order |
| Into the NT | No set hours inside the NT, but your operator must still manage fatigue under WHS law | Keep your diary going if you will leave the NT within seven days |
| Bass Strait to or from TAS | There is no special ferry rule. Check in, queueing, driving on and securing the truck are work. Time off the vehicle deck counts as stationary rest, because you are out of the truck. A normal overnight sailing can cover a 7 hour night rest break, but a late departure or early wake up call can cut it short. This reading of the HVNL comes from the research dossier: **Check before print** with the NHVR | Diary entries: work to rest when you leave the vehicle deck, rest to work when you return to it. Record the port and odometer each time, and keep all times in your base time zone. Book a cabin, not a recliner, if you need real sleep before driving off |

The NT guidance was written before 1 August 2026 and still names BFM and AFM. Read those as the new ACH options until the NT updates it.

### Border Day Checklist

- [ ] Diary up to date, with the last major rest break recorded
- [ ] Licence, medical certificate (WA) and accreditation details in the folder
- [ ] Permits and notices for every state on the route
- [ ] Alcohol limit for the state ahead checked (Chapter 2)
- [ ] Speed limit for your vehicle class in the state ahead checked (Chapter 6)
- [ ] Someone knows your route and arrival time if you are heading remote
